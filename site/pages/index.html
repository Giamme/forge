One command. Many models. Every diff reviewed.

forge is a skill for your coding agent. It plans your goal into tasks, sends each one to the model and CLI you choose, has a second model read every real diff, and merges only what passes.

forge reads the diff, not the claim

Every builder model says it's done. forge doesn't take its word. A different model, the reviewer, is handed the real diff and has to break it. Here is a real kind of catch, on demo code.

Nothing runs until you approve the table

Ask for a parallel run and forge splits the goal, rates each task by the judgment it needs, and routes it by your rules. Then it stops and shows you this. Retier a row and its models change. Approve, and the waves run.

Own worktreeEach row runs alone in its own git worktree on forge/a1c4/<task>, with a fresh session and a brief of what already merged.

WavesWave 2 waits for wave 1 to merge; tasks run together only when they own different files, and forge says why one waits.

Steer it while it runs

A run can take an hour. You stay in charge the whole time: answer the questions only you can answer, resize it, pin a model, retry, or accept a task the reviewer won't let go, from the terminal or from forge-ui.

  • Infra isn't failureA rate limit, quota, sign-in or network stop spends no attempt. forge pauses only the model or CLI that's stuck, moves sideways to a healthy one in the same tier, or waits it out.

Measured on your code, not on benchmarks

forge remembers what each run taught it about your repository, and records every run's inputs and outcomes outside your repos. Over time you know which models pass first time on your code, and what a reviewer's PASS was worth.

# .forge/memory.md  (small, capped, given to every run)
## Verify
- pytest -q runs the suite; make test also lints and is 4x slower

## Known traps
- src/proto/*_pb2.py is generated, never hand-edit  [src/proto]

## Recurring QA findings
- 3x: route added without updating openapi.yaml  [src/routes]

The model that hit the landmine writes it down. A review finding has to show up in two runs before it counts, a fact about a deleted file is dropped, and the file never grows past 40 lines.

What forge never does

forge works next to your code, not on top of it. Every run keeps these.

  • It never pushes.There is no push step. Your remote changes only when you push.
  • It never commits to your branch.Parallel work lives on branches forge makes for itself until you run integrate --approved. A single task's change stays uncommitted.
  • It never picks a model for you.Every dispatch spends your quota. Leave out the builder and forge asks.
  • It never merges unreviewed code.Only the exact commit a reviewer passed can merge. Overriding a reviewer takes a written reason and --approved.
  • It never deletes a failed attempt.The branch and worktree stay: the record of what went wrong and the place to retry from.
  • It never drops a sandbox on its own.Only --yolo-dwarf or --yolo-qa lets a role out, and only that role.
